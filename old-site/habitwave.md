## TL;DR
A side project started during lockdown to make habit-building more engaging by adding a social layer to habit tracking. About 600+ users in 23 countries.

## Prologue
Vinit met Charmie through an Instagram story looking for a UX designer for "a fun project". Vedant (developer) joined and the team explored problem spaces.

## Problem
People start habits but struggle to keep them. During COVID-19 lockdowns many picked up hobbies and gave up within days. User interviews validated assumptions and pain points.

## Research — user interviews
Participants: mostly professionals in their 20s who had recently started a habit. Interviews covered progress, tracking methods and obstacles.
[img] https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/61acfa305e678b4d4360930b/1ef986da-d7b8-486f-a99a-c5e447c05892/Frame+657.jpg
Insights: existing apps focus on daily check-offs. Fabulous, Habitica and Habitnow motivate through vocal coaching, avatar unlocks and data analysis. The team chose a support-system, social angle.
[img] https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/61acfa305e678b4d4360930b/a80d5c94-42e9-4843-ab5d-b8c06ec16083/Frame+656+%281%29.jpg

## Solution — V1.0 (MVP)
A quick proof of concept for early feedback: a basic habit tracker plus a squad page showing friends' habits.
[img ×5] "Habitwave //MVP" Frame 17–21. These are embedded in a gallery that the tool couldn't read the addresses of. Save them manually.

## Re-assessing V1.0
Two cohorts interviewed after a few weeks:
- Cohort 1, active users: feedback resembled Play Store reviews.
- Cohort 2, logged five or fewer times and dropped off: liked the app but got bored because their friends weren't on it.

## Learnings
- Strong research still may not predict real usage. Launching with enough research and then iterating on feedback worked well.
- Designers gave valuable feedback when approached directly.
- Doing this alongside a demanding full-time job was energizing and confirmed the passion for product design.
